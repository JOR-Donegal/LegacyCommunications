# 4. Leased Lines

The phone network was designed for analogue signals and to use the same wires for data, we had to use modulators/de-modulators or modems. For domestic use, we had dial-up modems which shared phone lines. For WAN use, we had phone lines dedicated for modem use only. The carrier provided a modem as CPE with a phone connection outbound and a serial digital interface inbound; these were provided at both ends of a link. This was a point-to-point lease line. The advantage of this technology is that it used a fixed, always available 64kbps channel, dedicated entirely to the customer and the link. Because these links were dedicated, they were reliable and consistent, and we still see them used in critical infrastructures. 

Typically, the carrier uses 2 or 4 wires for signaling and installs a modem at either end of the link. Once the signal gets to the exchange, it is sent on dedicated channels over the PDH/SDH network and out to the modem at the other side. These circuits are generally priced based on bandwidth required and distance between the two connected points. The downside of this network is that it permanently ties up lines, hardware, and channels and for this reason, is very expensive. The last 64kbps link we used in Donegal cost over €10,000 a year to operate, back when a house cost <€100k.
