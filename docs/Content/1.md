# 1. Technology Evolution
At time of writing, most of the world has access to ubiquitous, packet-based networking.

Original WAN technology was based on the ubiquitous network of its time, the telephone network.

In the late 1800s, data communications signals were analogue, and telephones were allocated a single channel per line for transmission. And before you ask, yes, we have electricity in Buncrana at that time as well. Do some background reading on the connection between the Swann family, the lightbulb, and early electrical generation. 

Once the vacuum tube came along, more complex techniques became available such as frequency division multiplexing (FDM). Coaxial cables were used, and following military developments in World War II, microwave telecommunications emerged. From the 1960s, and with the development of pulse code modulation (PCM), digital telephony became feasible. Digital systems using Time Division Multiplexing (TDM) were developed, and multi-megabit communication links began to be installed, primarily using fiber optic networks. 

The slightly tinny sound of a voice through the plain old telephone system (POTS) was the result of filters, restricting the voice signal to a bandpass of 300Hz-3.4KHz. Nyquist’s Theorem requires us to sample any analogue signal at twice the rate of its highest frequency component. If we assume a maximum frequency of 4 kHz, then our sampling frequency must be 8kHz. We can provide 256 discrete levels using eight bits, and in implementation, use compression algorithms to improve comprehensibility. 8-bit samples taken at 8 kHz results in a bit rate of 64 kb per second. This was the bandwidth used by design on all digital telephone systems and when telephone companies began to sell leased lines, they were sold in increments of 64 kb/s. 
In the mid-80's the most common digital hierarchy in use was Plesciosynchronous Digital Hierarchy (PDH). It is called 'plescio' (almost) because the transmission is neither wholly synchronous or asynchronous. 

PDH, was superseded by Synchronous Digital hierarchy (SDH) and Synchronous Optical Networking (SONET), which took the PDH signals and multiplexed them into Synchronous Time-Division Multiplexing (STM).
