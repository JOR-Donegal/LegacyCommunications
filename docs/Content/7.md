# Cell Switched Networks
These were a variation on circuit switched networks, where fixed sized packets (=cells) are transmitted as rapidly and simply as possible (e.g. unreliable) across virtual circuits. A typical technology was asynchronous transfer mode (ATM).

## Asynchronous Transfer Mode (ATM)
A successor was required to ISDN with higher bandwidth and with capabilities making it suitable for LAN extension and supporting video. The International Telecommunications Union (ITU) defined Asynchronous Transfer Mode (ATM) as the vehicle for Broadband ISDN with a view to it becoming the universal network transport. Different from TDM, time slots were available on demand with information identifying the source of the transmission contained in the header of each ATM cell. 

It mitigated all the known problems with protocols like Frame Relay and Ethernet and during the 1990s, it appeared that it would be become the emergent protocol. However, with the development of gigabit Ethernet, the industry fixed the underlying problems of latency and quality of service by throwing enormous bandwidth at the problem. That, as well as the economies of scale of Ethernet led to the eventual decline of ATM. 

ATM was complex to engineer, especially in the LAN where various emulations were used. In used SVCs and PVCs and four quality of service rates were introduced, making traffic management very effective.
