# 5. Circuit Switched Networks

As every link in a telephone network is brought up on demand as a point-to-point circuit, we refer to this as a circuit switched network. Switched circuits allow data connections that can be initiated when needed and terminated when communication is complete. This works much like a normal telephone line works for voice communication. Integrated Services Digital Network (ISDN) is a good example of circuit switching. When a router has data for a remote site, the switched circuit is initiated with the circuit number of the remote network. In the case of ISDN circuits, the device places a call to the telephone number of the remote ISDN circuit. When the two networks are connected and authenticated, they can transfer data. When the data transmission is complete, the call can be terminated. 

An ISDN Basic rate connection allows for 2*64kbps channels and a 16-kbps channel for signaling. 

There is also a primary rate ISDN. This is the equivalent of 32 channels of 64 kbps, with 30 channels usable for traffic and two channels utilized for signaling. This circuit has a total bit rate of 2.048 Mbps.
